User guide

Olupan is an offline workout log: log sets during your workout, body data, steps, food and stats – no account, all data on your device.

Install

iPhone, Safari: Share → Add to Home Screen. Android, Chrome: menu → Install app. It then starts from its own icon, full screen, even offline. On iPhone the home-screen app and Safari use separate storage – stick to one.

Getting started

Cards on the home screen guide you through setup: 1. profile (sex, birth year, height, activity and your current body weight; body fat is optional) – the suggested daily calorie target is calculated from this right away; 2. daily step goal (can be skipped); 3. food log with Claude: first your food preferences, then the Claude project following the guide. If you want your own calorie target, use the link under the suggestion. Open Claude in the same browser you use Olupan in – the Claude app has no web address to copy.

Home screen

The app opens on Today: the days of the week at the top (tap one to switch to that day), then the daily calorie budget and macros, measurements, steps and the day’s workout. Tap a card to open detailed logging. The Today button at top left opens the calendar; the icons at top right open stats and settings. The ‹ › buttons on the cards jump to the previous or next entry.

Workouts

  • Plan and start: on the home screen’s workout card you can pick the day’s workout (Plan for today) and start it from there. If no template fits, Build a new workout lets you put one together right there: on save it’s stored as a template and set for that day. On the Workouts and templates page you can also start an empty workout. An empty workout is also saved as a template under My workouts when finished.
  • Sets: enter weight and reps (distance and time for cardio), then check the set. The “Previous” column shows your last session.
  • Rest time: starts when you check a set; tap the time under an exercise name to change it. The screen stays awake during workouts; if you put the app in the background, you get a notification when rest ends (if allowed).
  • Set type: tap a set number (the button with a dashed border) to cycle it: B warm-up → D drop → F failure → normal. Warm-ups don’t count towards volume, records or set counts; D and F are working sets, only the label differs.
  • Warm-up calculator: for barbell exercises the Plates button, for dumbbell, machine and kettlebell exercises the Warm-up button suggests ramping warm-up sets from your working weight; tap a row to leave it out, then add them with one tap. If you switch them all off, the button removes the warm-ups not yet ticked.
  • Deleting a set: on a phone, swipe the row left and tap the red bin button; on a computer, hover over the row and the × appears. You can undo for a few seconds after deleting.
  • Exercises: each exercise has a measurement (weight × reps, reps with added weight, time, cardio), a primary muscle group, optional secondary muscles and equipment, chosen with pills; the stats are calculated from the muscle groups.
  • Suggestion and plates: for each exercise the app suggests a weight and reps based on last time (Fill in enters them); for barbell exercises Plates shows what to load per side.
  • Supersets: link an exercise with the next one using the chain icon at the bottom of its card; rest starts only after the round.
  • Training plan with Claude: on the Workouts and templates page, Request a plan builds a request (goal, days, equipment, experience and your exercise list); add the code block from the reply as templates with Paste. The request goes to the same Claude project as your food log, so the buttons become active once you’ve added the project link.
  • Templates: on the Workouts and templates page, templates are grouped (e.g. Gym, Bodyweight, Kettlebell, Cardio, Claude plans, Earlier workouts); tap a group to see its templates. The Tabata (HIIT) – bodyweight template has 8 rounds per exercise with 20 s of work and 10 s of rest.
  • Workout history: the Workouts and templates page shows your last 3 workouts; the Workout history link (also at the bottom of the home screen) opens a separate page with earlier ones, 12 at a time up to 60. Older ones are in the calendar. The Back button always takes you to where you opened the page from.
  • Running workout: runs full screen; the ‹ Today button takes you back to the home screen, where a card shows its progress.
  • Finish: only checked sets are saved. The app tells you about new records.

Body and steps

Tap the Body card on the home screen to log weight, body fat, waist and steps daily. On iPhone, steps, weight and body fat can also be imported from the Health app with a Shortcuts shortcut – see the guide under the ? button.

Food

Fill in your profile (Settings → Profile and goals) to get a suggested daily energy and macro budget. The card strip on the Food page scrolls sideways: Search, Quick log, With Claude, What else today?, My recipes and Barcode. Search covers about 650 basic foods and ready-made products: enter the amount in grams, millilitres or portions (e.g. 1 glass, 1 piece) and the calories, macros and fibre are filled in. Values come from the USDA database (using the European calorie method, carbohydrates excluding fibre as on EU labels) or, for Hungarian products, from estimated market averages. If your product differs (e.g. a light version), edit the per-100 g or per-100 ml values: it is also saved as your own item, which you can edit or delete under Settings → Food → My foods. For alcoholic drinks the energy from alcohol (7 kcal/g) is included in the calories. Quick log re-adds an earlier meal or a frequent item; its search covers all your earlier items. Barcode scans a package barcode (or you can type the number): it first looks in your own saved products, then online in the Open Food Facts open database. If nothing is found, you enter the label values once and the product is saved with its barcode, so next time it is found offline.

With Claude: every Claude action works the same way. Open in Claude copies the request the app has put together (if there is one) and opens your Claude project. Paste the request there, or for food logging just photograph or describe what you ate. When you come back, a bar at the top shows what the app is waiting for; copy the code block at the end of the reply and take it over with the bar’s Paste button. Paste recognises what arrived – a food log, recipes, a weekly menu, a training plan or a shared link – and puts each in its place. The guide to setting up the project is on the With Claude card and under Settings → Food; Open Claude lets you sign in or sign up in the browser.

My recipes and weekly menu: Recipe ideas asks for 15 seasonal ideas (breakfast, mains, light dinners, soups, salads, desserts), and on paste the recipes go straight into My recipes. There you can search by dish, ingredient and cuisine, use the quick row to filter by meal or favourites, and the Filters button for dish type, style and cuisine. On a recipe’s page you can rescale the ingredients to any number of servings, vote (liked ones are your favourites), add a note, put the ingredients on the shopping list, and log the servings you ate with Log – more accurate than an estimate. For a Weekly menu you choose the start day, how many days, how many days a week you can cook (1, 2, 3 or every day) and which meals; your target, preferences, favourite recipes and planned training days go into the request. The pasted menu is shown day by day, marking cooking and leftovers and the planned calories per day; its new recipes are added to My recipes. The shopping list comes from Claude’s summary: identical products merged, with pack-size hints, grouped by supermarket section (fruit and veg, meat and fish, dairy, bakery, dry goods, tins, spices and baking, frozen); spices and basics you probably have are collapsed at the bottom so you can check them with one tap. Claude also gives a guide for each cooking day: which recipes to make, what to start when, what to do meanwhile and how to store everything; on a cooking day it also shows on the home screen. Lists built from your own recipes are grouped too, with notes stripped and identical items merged. Today’s menu also appears on the home screen’s Food card and on the Food page, where you can log it with one tap. There is one shopping list: identical ingredients are added up, deleting a recipe removes only its items, and you can add your own; while it has items, it is also reachable from the home screen header. What else today? asks what you have at hand or where you are, and suggests only from that within today’s remaining budget; you just read the reply in Claude. You can share a recipe (on its page) or up to 5 workout templates (on the Workouts and templates page) by link or QR code: the content is in the link, nothing goes to a server, and the recipient imports it after a preview. On iPhone the installed app uses separate storage from Safari, so paste the link inside the app (My recipes → Paste a reply or link), or scan the QR code with the app’s barcode reader.

Adaptive target: once you have logged at least 2 weeks of meals and weigh-ins, Settings → Profile and goals estimates your actual energy needs from intake and weight trend, and suggests a weekly target adjustment (at most 250 kcal at a time).

Food preferences: under Settings → Food → Food preferences you can set your diet (e.g. dairy-free, vegan), foods to avoid, cuisines (e.g. Italian, Thai, Moroccan – for the weekly menu the app picks 1–2 of them, recipe ideas use all; for hard-to-find ingredients Claude names a substitute next to the original), style (e.g. budget-friendly, make-ahead; Hands-off means dishes with little active work that cook “by themselves”), your cooking level, other wishes in free text (e.g. Indian-style, kosher), and your kitchen equipment. If you tick any equipment, Claude uses only that. Claude requests are available once your profile is filled in, because your daily targets go into them.

Calendar and stats

The Calendar (the Today button at top left) marks days with workouts, measurements or food; tap a day to switch the home screen to it. Stats has weight and body fat trends, energy balance, steps, workout count, volume, strength trends, personal records, weekly sets and frequency per muscle group, a muscle distribution radar chart (last 4 weeks vs previous 4), relative strength (estimated 1RM ÷ body weight) with suggestions, and a combined trend (the detailed view’s period selector – 30 days, 90 days, 1 year, all – stays under the header while you scroll) showing what moves together with your weight.

Backup, export, import

  • Summary for my coach: at the top of the Stats page. One screen for the chosen period (2, 4 or 8 weeks): average calories and macros against your targets, days on target, weight trend and weekly change, body fat, waist, average steps, workouts per week, weekly volume, sets per muscle group, estimated 1RM change on your main lifts and the period’s records. You can add a note; at a session just show it, or save it as a PDF from the print menu.
  • Backup: Settings → Data → Save / share – put the file on iCloud, Google Drive or in Files. With no server, this is the only way to restore everything on a new device: back up regularly. If you haven’t backed up for a while, the home screen reminds you; the frequency (daily, weekly – the default –, every two weeks, off) is set in the same place. The backup can be encrypted with a password (Set up encryption): the app doesn’t store the password and you don’t have to type it for later backups, but it asks for it now and then as a check, and always when restoring. Without the password an encrypted backup can’t be restored – write it down or keep it in a password manager. Generate a passphrase picks 6 words from the Hungarian Diceware word list.
  • Sample data: on first launch you can try the app with sample data from the profile notice card. Before using the app for real, delete it with the Delete button on the card (or under Settings → Data); your settings and templates stay.
  • CSV export: workouts, body data, steps and food as spreadsheets.
  • Import: restore a backup file, or import workouts from CSV – the Sample button gives you an example file.

Settings

Grouped by topic: profile and goals, appearance (theme, accent colour, language), workout (rest time, sounds, notifications, keep awake, exercises), food, health, data and about. From a submenu, ‹ Settings takes you back, and from there ‹ Today returns to the home screen.
